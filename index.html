About

I'm a human-centered AI researcher trained in psychology and HCI, studying how people perceive, trust, and emotionally connect with AI-driven robots, virtual agents, and other artificial communication partners. My work combines embodied interaction design, controlled experimentation, and multimodal behavioral analysis — gesture, movement synchrony, and other nonverbal signal channels — to understand, and ultimately help engineer, emotionally attuned human–machine communication.

My current research identifies how nonverbal behavioral synchrony predicts felt interpersonal connection in mediated (VR) communication, building toward a broader research agenda on affective and social intelligence in embodied AI and robotic systems. I bring a rigorous mixed-methods approach — experimental design, statistical analysis (R, Python, SPSS), and qualitative thematic analysis — to this work. I'm based in Tokyo. English and Mandarin are my native languages, and I'm JLPT N2 in Japanese, progressing toward N1.

In preparation · CHI 2027

Behavioral Synchrony in VR Communication

How nonverbal alignment predicts felt interpersonal connection — first author

A controlled VR study (N=44 English/Japanese dyads) comparing face-to-face and back-to-back conversation conditions, with 60+ semi-structured interviews across both language groups.

Finding: behavioral synchrony significantly predicts felt interpersonal connection (r=.31, p=.003) — direct evidence that nonverbal signals shape how connected people feel to a communication partner.
