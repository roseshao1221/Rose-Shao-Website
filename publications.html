Heads Up: Tracing Head-Motion Coherence and Connection in Social VR

Submitted to IEEE VR · Under review

A controlled Social VR study of 44 participants in English- and Japanese-language dyads, comparing face-to-face and back-to-back conversation orientations through head-and-face-tracked avatars. The paper combines head-motion coherence, self-report, and interview data to examine how sparse embodiment shapes behavioral coordination and felt connection.

Finding: visual orientation affected head-motion structure and subjective experience in parallel, but coherence did not independently index felt connection. The paper argues for dyad-level inference, interaction-specific controls, and construct validation in Social VR telemetry studies.

Honorable Mention · AH 2026 Third author

A Design Framework for Virtual Space based on Spectral Relationships

Proceedings of the Augmented Humans International Conference 2026 (pp. 141–152), ACM

Proposed "Spectral Space," a framework for multilayer continuous virtual environments organized by spectral (frequency-domain) relationships between entities, letting users navigate smoothly among multiple coexisting worlds. I designed the study methodology and interview protocol, conducted the user study, and led qualitative analysis of participant interviews.

Finding: in a comparative study against conventional scene-based transition techniques, Spectral Space maintained presence and self-location while significantly increasing usable spatial range, promoting exploratory behavior, and supporting layered interpretation of objects and worlds.

Honorable Mention · AH 2025 Second author

Cuddle-Fish: Exploring a Soft Floating Robot with Flapping Wings for Physical Interactions

Proceedings of the Augmented Humans International Conference 2025 (pp. 160–173), ACM

Contributed to the design and evaluation of Cuddle-Fish, a soft floating robot with flapping wings built to explore empathy and emotional connection through physical, embodied interaction rather than screen-based interfaces. I designed and led a mixed-methods study (24 participants), conducting interviews and thematic analysis of participants' behavioral and emotional responses.

Outcome: findings on empathy formation between humans and the robot produced evidence-based recommendations that directly informed the robot's interaction design.
